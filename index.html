How I see marketing

  1. 1Understand before you act.

  4. 4Be there when they're ready.
  5. 5Pipeline is the measure.

Marketing isn't a lead factory. But it is accountable for pipeline.

Three problems I've met more than once.

Pick one. The obvious answer sits above the line. The thinking sits below it.

“We need more leads.”

The problemPipeline looks thin next quarter.

  1. The tempting answer

    Spend more on campaigns.

  2. beneath
  3. Worth asking

    Where do deals stall? Which leads did sales accept?

  4. The pattern

    Plenty come in. They drop before a sales conversation.

  5. What it means

    More volume would only widen the leak.

  6. The decision

    One shared lead definition. Fix the drop-off. Report pipeline.

  7. Where I met itNet Solutions →

“Buyers see us as just a vendor.”

The problemBuyers file the company under outsourcing.

  1. The tempting answer

    Refresh the logo and the website.

  2. beneath
  3. Worth asking

    Which category are we in? Who are we compared with?

  4. The pattern

    The category decides the comparison.

  5. What it means

    A new look on the same story changes nothing.

  6. The decision

    Change the category. Then prove it in public.

  7. Where I met itCogneesol →

“Growth costs more every month.”

The problemEach new user costs more than the last.

  1. The tempting answer

    Raise the acquisition budget.

  2. beneath
  3. Worth asking

    Who are our users, really? Who do they talk to?

  4. The pattern

    Users cluster in cohorts. And they talk to each other.

  5. What it means

    Bought growth stops when spend does.

  6. The decision

    Cohort messaging, plus a referral programme.

  7. Where I met itPrepLadder →
