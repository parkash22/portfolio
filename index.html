How I see marketing

One connected loop, not a lead factory. Every part feeds the next, and pipeline is where it gets counted.

  1. 1Start with understanding.Most channel problems are really understanding problems: who buys, and what worries them.
  2. 2Brand and revenue are one job.How you're perceived decides whether you're considered at all. It's more than a logo.
  3. 3Earn attention before you ask for it.Useful ideas, a clear point of view and proof from others do the persuading.
  4. 4Be there when they're ready.Search, AI answers and focused accounts catch the demand you helped create.
  5. 5Pipeline is the measure.Marketing isn't a lead factory, but it is accountable for pipeline. That only works when sales and marketing share one definition.
  6. 6Data informs. People decide.A dashboard isn't a decision. It tells you what to look at next.

And none of it works in a quarter. Consistency is what makes it compound.

Thinking, one frame at a time.

Three problems I've met more than once. The first two frames are what's visible on the surface. The rest is the work underneath.

“We need more leads.”

On the surfaceBeneath
  1. 01
    The problem

    Leadership wants more leads. Next quarter's pipeline looks thin.

  2. 02
    The tempting answer

    Not enough people are coming in. Spend more on campaigns.

  3. 03
    Questions worth asking

    Where do current deals stall? Which leads did sales accept? Which sources became pipeline, not just form fills?

  4. 04
    What the pattern showed

    Volume at the top is fine. Buyers drop between a first visit and a sales conversation, and sales and marketing define a good lead differently.

  5. 05
    What that means

    More volume would widen the leak. The fix is in the middle of the journey and in the handoff.

  6. 06
    The decision

    Agree one definition of a qualified lead with sales. Fix the drop-off points. Report pipeline, not form fills.

“Buyers see us as just a vendor.”

On the surfaceBeneath
  1. 01
    The problem

    Sales keeps hearing the same thing: you're an outsourcing vendor. The company can do far more than that.

  2. 02
    The tempting answer

    It's a design problem. Refresh the logo and the website.

  3. 03
    Questions worth asking

    Which category do buyers put us in? Which competitors and budgets does that category put us against? What proof would a buyer believe?

  4. 04
    What the pattern showed

    The category decides the comparison. Buyers discount what you say about yourself, and listen to analysts, peers and presence.

  5. 05
    What that means

    A new look on the same story gets filed in the same place. Change the story, then prove it in public.

  6. 06
    The decision

    Reposition from BPO to BPM and business solutions. Back it with analyst recognition, industry events, search and focused ABM.

“Growth costs more every month.”

On the surfaceBeneath
  1. 01
    The problem

    Each new user costs more to win than the one before.

  2. 02
    The tempting answer

    Raise the acquisition budget and buy the growth.

  3. 03
    Questions worth asking

    Who are our users, really? What happens after they sign up? Who are they already talking to?

  4. 04
    What the pattern showed

    Sales, marketing and support each hold a different piece of the user. Joined up, cohorts appear, each with its own moment of need. And users talk to each other.

  5. 05
    What that means

    Bought growth stops when the spend does. Growth that feeds itself compounds.

  6. 06
    The decision

    Join the data into cohorts, speak to each one at the right moment, and build a referral programme so users bring users.

PrepLadder · Medical exam-prep platform · 2017–20Digital Marketing Manager

Know the student. Then let students bring students.

6×85,000 to 500,000+ users in three years
85K at the start added · each dot is 5,000 users

My partRan digital marketing across the whole student lifecycle, with product, in-house doctors, creators and video teams.

Not a funnel. A loop.

Students don't buy once and leave. They prepare for months, compare notes, and tell each other what works. So the growth plan followed them, not a sales stage.

Context

An exam-prep platform for doctors, in a crowded EdTech market.

Product teamIn-house doctorsContent creatorsVideo teamsAgencies and freelancers
What I saw

Sales, marketing and support each knew something different about a student. Joined up, those records showed who students really were.

What we built

Brand, lifecycle and referral, built together.

Brand identityVoice and visual consistency, built from an early stage
Lifecycle automationSegmented, event-based messages tested by cohort
Referral programmeA structured way for students to bring students
Content with expertsCampaigns made with in-house doctors, creators and video teams
Outcome

Growth that fed itself, alongside paid acquisition: 500,000+ users, and referral sign-ups up 40%.

Off the record

I spent three years directing some of India's best doctors in front of a camera. They could explain the hardest topics in medicine without notes, then rehearsed a ten-second promo line like it was their final viva.

LessonExpertise and confidence on camera are different skills. Both can be coached.
